## 2. Authoritative Legal, Regulatory & Payment-Provider Requirements

To ensure complete legal compliance and financial viability, we audited governing frameworks in Bangladesh, primary consumer-protection statutes, and official payment gateway documentation:

### 2.1 Bangladesh Legal & Regulatory Framework
1. **The Consumers' Right Protection Act, 2009 (ভোক্তা অধিকার সংরক্ষণ আইন, ২০০৯ — Act No. 26 of 2009):**
   - *Statutory Basis:* Sections 37, 40, 44, 45, and 53 protect consumers against price inflation, deceptive advertising, and service failure:
     - *Section 40:* Prohibits selling services at a price higher than the fixed/displayed fee.
     - *Section 44:* Prohibits false or misleading advertisements designed to deceive purchasers.
     - *Section 45:* Prohibits failure to deliver promised goods or services after receiving payment.
     - *Section 53:* Establishes civil and penal liability for service failure causing financial damage to consumers.
   - *Statutory Cooling-Off Reality:* The Act does **not** mandate a statutory right of withdrawal or cooling-off period for instantly accessed digital streaming media or SaaS memberships. Any cooling-off period (such as the proposed 48-hour window) is a **contractual product policy** rather than a statutory mandate.
   - *Legal Effect:* Once published in the terms of service, cooling-off and cancellation commitments become legally binding under Sections 44 and 45. Arbitrarily refusing an eligible refund or secretly altering terms constitutes an actionable statutory offense.
2. **Digital Commerce Operation Guidelines, 2021 (ডিজিটাল কমার্স পরিচালনা নির্দেশিকা ২০২১ — Ministry of Commerce Gazette Notification No. 26.00.0000.112.014.004.2021-182, dated 4 July 2021):**
   - *Clause 3.1.4 (General Compliance & Disclosure):* Digital commerce platforms must conspicuously publish platform credentials, contact information (trade license, telephone, email, and physical address), product/service specifications, general terms, and return/refund policies on their website/application.
   - *Clause 3.1.5 (Product/Service Disclosure):* Full service descriptions, total payable prices, and all applicable delivery/gateway charges must be clearly displayed prior to purchase confirmation.
   - *Clause 3.1.9 (Prohibition on Unapproved Wallets/Vouchers):* Digital wallets, gift cards, cash vouchers, or alternative payment mediums cannot be issued, sold, or used without prior approval from **Bangladesh Bank**. Merchants are legally prohibited from trapping customer refunds in captive platform credits or vouchers when a refund is requested.
   - *Clauses 3.3.4, 3.3.6 & Section 3.5 (Delivery Failure & 10-Day Refund Mandate):* If an order is canceled or the seller fails to deliver the promised digital service, the merchant must disburse the full refund within **a maximum of 10 calendar days**.
   - *Mandatory Same-Medium Disbursement:* The refund must be credited strictly through the **exact same payment medium/channel** (Card, bKash, Nagad, bank account) through which the student originally paid.
3. **Bangladesh Bank National Payment Switch Bangladesh (NPSB) & E-Mandate Directives:**
   - Recurring merchant-initiated debits (MIT) require prior customer mandate consent with two-factor authentication (SMS/Email OTP) on initial registration.
   - Cardholders retain the legal right to dispute unauthorized recurring charges through their card-issuing bank under card association regulations.

### 2.2 Payment Gateway Rules & Fee Arithmetic (SSLCommerz)
1. **Refund API Endpoint:**
   - Official API: `GET /validator/api/merchantTransIDvalidationAPI.php`
   - Mandatory parameters: `bank_tran_id`, `refund_trans_id`, `refund_amount`, `refund_remarks`, `store_id`, `store_passwd`, `format=json`.
2. **Gateway Fee Arithmetic & Non-Refundability:**
   - Under Bangladesh tax regulations (Value Added Tax and Supplementary Duty Act, 2012 — Act No. 47 of 2012), payment gateway commissions charged by aggregators (PSOs/PSPs) are subject to **15% Value Added Tax (VAT)**.
   - **Contractual Status Warning:** TechSprout's specific contractual Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) schedule with SSLCommerz is an **UNCONFIRMED SCENARIO ASSUMPTION** awaiting formal written addendum approval (`P8.0_SSLCOMMERZ_MERCHANT_INQUIRY_AND_CHECKLIST.md` Item 10).
   - **Fee Breakdown for a ৳500.00 Transaction (50,000 poisha) under Evaluated Scenarios:**
     - *Scenario 1 (Corrected Card Baseline: 2.50% MDR + 15% VAT on fee = 2.875% Effective Rate):*
       - Base MDR (2.50%): $50,000 \times 0.025 = 1,250\text{ poisha}$ ($৳12.50$)
       - 15% VAT on MDR: $\text{round}(1,250 \times 0.15) = 188\text{ poisha}$ ($৳1.88$)
       - Total Sunk Fee: $1,250 + 188 = 1,438\text{ poisha}$ ($৳14.38$)
       - Net Cash Received: $50,000 - 1,438 = 48,562\text{ poisha}$ ($৳485.62$)
     - *Scenario 2 (Historical Unadjusted Baseline: 2.500% Flat / VAT Omitted):*
       - Total Sunk Fee: $1,250\text{ poisha}$ ($৳12.50$)
       - Net Cash Received: $48,750\text{ poisha}$ ($৳487.50$)
     - *Scenario 3 (Domestic Debit / MFS Rate: 2.00% MDR + 15% VAT on fee = 2.300% Effective Rate):*
       - Base MDR (2.00%): $1,000\text{ poisha}$ ($৳10.00$)
       - 15% VAT on MDR: $150\text{ poisha}$ ($৳1.50$)
       - Total Sunk Fee: $1,150\text{ poisha}$ ($৳11.50$)
       - Net Cash Received: $48,850\text{ poisha}$ ($৳488.50$)
   - **Non-Refundability upon Refund:** Under acquiring bank rules in Bangladesh, gateway MDR fees and VAT on fees are **strictly non-refundable**. When TechSprout issues a full $৳500.00$ refund, the platform permanently absorbs an unrecovered cash loss of **$৳14.38$ ($1,438\text{ poisha}$)** per transaction under Scenario 1 (or $৳12.50$ under Scenario 2; $৳11.50$ under Scenario 3).
3. **Chargeback Economics & Merchant Risk:**
   - Under Visa/Mastercard operating regulations (Chargeback Reason Code 10.4 / 4853 - Recurring Transaction Cancelled) and local acquiring bank agreements, disputed debits incur:
     - Immediate debit of the disputed transaction amount ($৳500.00$).
     - A non-refundable **dispute administration penalty fee of $৳1,000\text{ to }৳2,500$** per claim.
     - Acquiring bank penalties or merchant account termination if the platform chargeback ratio exceeds $0.9\%$.
   - *Strategic Implication:* Refusing accidental renewal refunds drives students to file bank disputes, creating financial liabilities up to $5\times$ greater than the subscription price.

## 4. Post-Settlement Refund Architecture: Platform Reserve Buffer vs. Instructor Clawback

A critical financial risk arises if a renewal refund, bank chargeback, or dispute occurs **after the calendar month has closed and revenue has been distributed to instructor wallets**.

### 4.1 Approach A: Direct Instructor Wallet Clawback (REJECTED)
- If a $৳500.00$ refund occurs post-settlement, reversing the 70% pool distribution requires clawing back funds from 4+ instructors proportionally.
- *Fatal Technical Failure:* If an instructor has already withdrawn their available balance, a debit transaction will violate the database check constraint `CHECK (available_balance_cents >= 0)`, causing transaction crashes or creating negative wallet balances.
- *Commercial Failure:* Retroactively deducting fractional poisha from instructor wallets over past months severely damages instructor trust.

### 4.2 Approach B: Platform Subscription Reserve Account (RECOMMENDED)
- **Mechanism:** The platform establishes a dedicated internal balance: `SUBSCRIPTION_RESERVE_ACCOUNT`.
- **Funding:** 5% of the platform's retained commission is allocated to this reserve each month ($৳688.41\text{ / month}$ at 100 subscribers under corrected 2.875% fee).
- **Settlement:** All post-settlement refunds, chargeback dispute losses, and gateway penalty fees are debited strictly from `SUBSCRIPTION_RESERVE_ACCOUNT`.
- **Integrity Guarantee:** Zero clawbacks from instructor wallets, zero database constraint violations, and complete double-entry ledger stability.
